


Four kinds of helpers:

- **Reactive** — a **thermostat**. Too cold → heater on. Too hot → heater off. It never thinks about the future. It just reacts to right now.
- **Deliberative** — a **chess player**. Before moving, they think: "if I do this, he does that, then I do this." They plan ahead, then act.
- **Goal-based** — a **treasure hunter with a map**. Before every step they ask: "does this get me closer to the X?" If yes, go. If no, try another way.
- **Learning** — a **kid learning to ride a bicycle**. First try: falls. Second try: falls less. Tenth try: rides. They get better from experience.

---

## Simple technical version

|Type|How it decides|Does it remember?|Does it plan ahead?|Does it improve over time?|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Reactive**|Fixed rules: "if X, do Y"|No|No|No|
|**Deliberative**|Builds a plan first, then follows it|Yes|Yes|No|
|**Goal-based**|Picks whatever action moves it toward the goal|Yes|A little or a lot|No|
|**Learning**|Same as above, but changes its behaviour from feedback|Yes|Depends|**Yes**|

These names come from the standard AI textbook (_Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach_ by Russell & Norvig). They're older than AI chat models, but the ideas still apply.

### Reactive vs Deliberative — the real trade-off

|Point|Reactive|Deliberative|
|---|---|---|
|Speed|Fast|Slow|
|Cost|Cheap|Expensive|
|Handles surprises|Very well (it only looks at now)|Badly (its plan can go stale)|
|Handles complex tasks|Badly|Well|

Many real systems are **hybrid**: they plan a little, but still react to what actually happens.

### Where AI agents fit

Most AI agents today are **goal-based hybrids**. The common pattern is called **ReAct** (short for _Reason + Act_):

```
think a little  →  do one action  →  look at result
        ↑                                   │
        └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

It doesn't make a full plan up front. It just decides **one next step at a time**, and looks at the result before deciding again. This is reactive _and_ goal-based at once.

### Important warning about "learning"

When people say an AI agent "learns," they usually **do not** mean the AI model itself changes.

|What people say|What actually happens|
|---|---|
|"It learned my preference"|Your preference was **saved as a note**, and that note gets pasted into the AI's input next time|
|"It got smarter"|The developers **tested and improved the code/prompts**, or trained a new model version offline|

The AI model's brain does **not** change while it's running. Saving notes and pasting them back is **memory**, not learning.

## Real-life examples

### How Claude does it

Inside one task, I'm a **goal-based hybrid**. I do a step, look at the result, then decide the next step. I don't write out a full plan for every task first.

I do **not** learn across conversations. When this chat ends, I won't remember it. If a memory feature is switched on, the app saves notes and pastes them into my input later — but that's the app remembering, not me. (In this chat, memory is off.)

### How ChatGPT does it

ChatGPT has a memory feature that saves facts about you and brings them into later chats. Same idea: notes saved and pasted back in, not the model retraining on you.

### In real products

- **Thermostat / basic robot vacuum bumper logic** — reactive. Rules only.
- **Self-driving car route planner** — deliberative. It plans a route, then re-plans when traffic changes.
- **Coding agents** — goal-based hybrid. Change code → run tests → read the failure → decide the next change.
- **Recommendation feeds** (YouTube, Instagram) — learning systems. They're retrained on what people click.

## Say it to your manager

> "Our agent is goal-based: it decides one step at a time based on the last result. We add full up-front planning only if our evaluation proves it actually performs better, because planning costs more time and money and adds new ways to fail."

---

## Common mistakes

- Thinking **memory = learning**. Saving notes is not the model getting smarter.
- Thinking deliberative (planning) is always better. It's slower, costs more, and its plan can be wrong from step one.
